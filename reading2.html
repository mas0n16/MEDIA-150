With 'AI slop' distorting our reality, the world is sleepwalking into disaster

Reading 2 Summary


Alternative intelligence is changing our perception of what is real. With AI making its way into politics and spreading like wildfire across social media platforms, it’s becoming more and more dangerous to be in a world where we constantly have to guess what is real and what is not. The article “With ‘AI Slop’ distorting our reality, the world is sleepwalking into disaster” by Nesrine Malik states that there are two parallel image channels that dominate our daily visual consumption. One contains real pictures and footage of the world as it is with politics, sports, news and entertainment. The other is AI slop as she calls it—low-quality content with minimal human input. When the latter was on the rise, it was much easier to grasp which was which, but as the technology builds upon itself, it is becoming increasingly difficult to refrain our minds from accepting what seems real. The result of this content is not just a blurring of reality, but a distortion of it. A new genre of this AI slop is rightwing political fantasy as she calls it. “There entire YouTube videos of made-up scenarios in which Trump officials prevail against liberal forces”. Trump has shared AI content of him and Netanyahu sipping cocktails in a ‘Trump Gaza' laid back on land that they destroyed to build resorts. To have this type of behavior facilitated by the sitting President is something we have never experienced before, and makes you wonder if there is a line to the lengths this kind of content will go. “The impulse behind this politicisation of AI is not new; it is simply an extension of traditional propaganda”. Propaganda itself is not a new concept introduced by AI, though it is vastly accelerated and more dangerous due to the reason that you can trick people’s minds to believe whatever you want them to. There are an infinite number of fictional scenarios at the disposal of both good and bad forces. Most of this AI slop is not as serious as the situations I described, but the effects could be just as impactful. Most of the time, it is just content-farming chaos–exaggerated or sensationalised material like animals dancing, but the outcome is an internet of robots, tickling human users into whatever feelings and passions keep them engaged. To social media companies like Facebook, this kind of content is ideal. It is highly engaging and less costly to produce than paying humans to make content. Whatever the intent may be behind AI content, it all leads to the desensitisation and overwhelming of visual palates. Being exposed to crazy outlandish material on a daily basis creates a world of consumers who don’t have a reaction to our government employing agencies to kill people who don’t agree with them or look like them. The result of AI is detrimental to the way we see the world, and what we can do to prevent and act against the disservice we are up against. It is increasingly important to stay skeptical of anything that pops up on the screens that we have so dearly integrated into our lives.
